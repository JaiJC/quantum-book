Cross sections and decay rates

A theorist writes ∣M∣2|\mathcal M|^2∣M∣2 on a blackboard. Two years later, an experiment at the Large Hadron Collider reports a few hundred events in a narrow bump. Between the two lies a short chain of formulas: integrate over the ways the products can fly apart, divide by how fast the beams approach, and multiply by how many collisions the machine delivers. This chapter is that chain, and at the end of it you will predict how long a muon lives.

The muon’s lifetime, predicted

A muon decays to an electron and two neutrinos through the weak force. Treating the force as a contact interaction with Fermi’s constant GF=1.166×10−5G_F = 1.166\times10^{-5}GF​=1.166×10−5 GeV⁻², the three-body phase space integral gives

Γμ=GF2 mμ5192π3\Gamma_\mu = \frac{G_F^2\,m_\mu^5}{192\pi^3}Γμ​=192π3GF2​mμ5​​

With mμ=105.66m_\mu = 105.66mμ​=105.66 MeV this is 3.01×10−193.01\times10^{-19}3.01×10−19 GeV, a lifetime of 2.187 microseconds. The measured value is 2.197 µs. The half-percent gap is filled by QED corrections and the electron’s mass. The fifth power of the mass is worth noticing: a particle twice as heavy decays 32 times faster. The tau lepton, 17 times heavier, lives about 17517^5175 times shorter, less a correction for its extra decay routes.
