Density matrices

Take one electron from a pair in the singlet state of Chapter 41 and carry it away. What is its state? Not up, not down, not any direction on the Bloch sphere: every direction you measure gives a perfect fifty-fifty split, which no wavefunction for a single spin can do. Your half of an entangled pair has no wavefunction of its own. To describe it, and every real system that is coupled to anything else, quantum mechanics needs a more general object: the density matrix.

On the plate: two entangled spins. The one you hold, on the left, sits at the very centre of its sphere.

Pure and mixed

Suppose a source sends spin-up with probability p1p_1p1​ and spin-down with probability p2p_2p2​, and you don’t know which. Every prediction is an average over the possibilities, and it can be written with one matrix:

ρ=∑ipi ∣ψi⟩⟨ψi∣,⟨A⟩=Tr(ρA)\rho = \sum_i p_i\,|\psi_i\rangle\langle\psi_i|, \qquad \langle A\rangle = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho A)ρ=i∑​pi​∣ψi​⟩⟨ψi​∣,⟨A⟩=Tr(ρA)

A pure state is the special case with one term, ρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣\rho = |\psi\rangle\langle\psi|ρ=∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣. A mixed state has more. The test is the purity Tr ρ2\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho^2Trρ2: one for pure states, less for mixed ones. John von Neumann introduced ρ\rhoρ in 1927. Note the difference from a superposition: (∣↑⟩+∣↓⟩)/2(|{\uparrow}\rangle + |{\downarrow}\rangle)/\sqrt2(∣↑⟩+∣↓⟩)/2​ is pure and definitely spin-up along xxx; an equal mixture of up and down is not up along any axis. The Bloch ball

Every spin-½ density matrix can be written ρ=12(1+r⋅σ)\rho = \tfrac12(\mathbb 1 + \mathbf r\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)ρ=21​(1+r⋅σ) with ∣r∣≤1|\mathbf r| \le 1∣r∣≤1. Pure states sit on the surface of the Bloch sphere of Chapter 40; mixed states fill its inside. Shrink the vector to length 0.70 at tilt 50°.

The purity is (1+r2)/2(1 + r^2)/2(1+r2)/2. The centre, ρ=1/2\rho = \mathbb 1/2ρ=1/2, is the maximally mixed state: complete ignorance. The off-diagonal entries of ρ\rhoρ, the coherences, carry the ability to interfere, and in the next chapters you will watch them leak away.

Different stories, same matrix

An equal mixture of up and down gives ρ=1/2\rho = \mathbb 1/2ρ=1/2. So does an equal mixture of +x+x+x and −x-x−x, and so does an equal mixture of three states at 120° around any great circle. Try , or .

No measurement can tell these preparations apart, because every prediction depends only on ρ\rhoρ. This is not a technicality. It is why, in Chapter 50, entanglement cannot be used to send a message.

Throwing away half: the partial trace

For two systems in a pure joint state, the state of one alone is found by summing over the other’s possibilities: the partial trace, ρA=TrB ∣Ψ⟩⟨Ψ∣\rho_A = \mathrm{Tr}_B\,|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|ρA​=TrB​∣Ψ⟩⟨Ψ∣. Take ∣Ψ⟩=cos⁡θ ∣00⟩+sin⁡θ ∣11⟩|\Psi\rangle = \cos\theta\,|00\rangle + \sin\theta\,|11\rangle∣Ψ⟩=cosθ∣00⟩+sinθ∣11⟩ and set θ=\theta =θ= 20°.

At 0° the state is a product and each half is pure. As θ\thetaθ grows, each half’s Bloch vector shrinks, to length cos⁡2θ\cos 2\thetacos2θ, and at 45°, a Bell state, it reaches the centre. The whole is perfectly known; each part is completely uncertain. The entropy of either half, S=−Tr ρAlog⁡2ρAS = -\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_A\log_2\rho_AS=−TrρA​log2​ρA​, measures the entanglement in bits: one bit for a Bell pair.

A spin in warm water

In thermal equilibrium a system is in the mixed state ρ=e−H/kBT/Z\rho = e^{-H/k_BT}/Zρ=e−H/kB​T/Z (Chapter 19). For a proton spin in a field, the Bloch vector points along the field with length tanh⁡(ℏγB/2kBT)\tanh(\hbar\gamma B/2k_BT)tanh(ℏγB/2kB​T). Set the scanner to 3.0 T at body temperature.

At 3 T the polarization is about ten parts per million: in every million protons in your body, only about ten more point along the field than against it. MRI works with that tiny excess. Techniques that hyperpolarize spins, pushing the vector far closer to the surface, can raise signals by factors of ten thousand and more, and are used to image metabolism in tumours.

Every mixture is part of something pure

Any mixed state can be obtained by tracing out part of a larger pure state: add a partner system and entangle it appropriately. This is purification. It means a mixed state can always be read in two ways: as ignorance of which pure state was prepared, or as entanglement with something you are not looking at. Quantum mechanics does not say which reading is right; the mathematics is the same. That ambiguity is at the heart of the measurement problem in Chapter 52.
