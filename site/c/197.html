Quantum simulation of electron transfer and Marcus theory

Marcus theory (Chapter 163) treats the environment’s motions classically. That works when the relevant vibrations are slow and thermally excited. But high-frequency molecular vibrations, carbon–carbon and carbon–oxygen stretches with quanta far above kBTk_BTkB​T, cannot be treated classically, and they change the inverted region dramatically. In 2024 a trapped-ion experiment simulated electron transfer with a tunable environment, reproducing these regimes on demand. This chapter shows the physics and the experiment.

In December 2024 Visal So, Guido Pagano and colleagues at Rice University published in Science Advances a trapped-ion simulation of electron transfer, encoding donor and acceptor in an ion’s spin and the environment in its motion, with dissipation engineered by laser cooling, and measured transfer rates across normal and inverted regimes. Established for the demonstration.

The critique

A single spin coupled to one damped mode is classically solvable, and Marcus–Levich–Jortner theory already describes most experiments. The value of analog simulators lies in regimes classical methods find hard: strong, structured coupling to many modes, non-Markovian memory, and multi-site networks where coherent and incoherent transfer compete. Those regimes do matter for biology, for instance in photosynthetic reaction centres and multi-cofactor chains, but no simulation has yet produced a biological result classical methods could not. Speculative for advantage.
