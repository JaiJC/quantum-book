Derive-from-scratch exam: QFT

The second exam covers quantum field theory, from relativistic wave equations to the running of couplings. These derivations are longer, and a few take a full page of Dirac traces. That is the point: QFT’s results only feel real once you have pushed through the algebra yourself. Ten questions, about four hours, no notes. The plate holds the checked results.

On the plate: the questions and their results.

Rules and rubric

Same rubric as Chapter 209: setup 3, method 4, result 2, check 1 per question, out of 100. Useful checks: Lorentz invariance of the final expression, the correct mass dimension, gauge invariance (amplitudes vanish when a photon polarization is replaced by its momentum), and the high-energy limit. Natural units, ℏ=c=1\hbar = c = 1ℏ=c=1, throughout. Established as method.

Checking traces by computer

For questions 6 and 7, after deriving by hand, check your traces with a computer algebra system: the book’s own checks used SymPy’s gamma-matrix module. Disagreement usually means a missing factor of 2 from spin sums or averages, or a sign from the metric. Hunting it down is how the technique becomes yours. Established as method.
