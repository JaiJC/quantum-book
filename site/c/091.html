Dark matter and dark energy

Everything in the Standard Model, every atom, star and neutrino, makes up about 5% of the energy in the universe. About 27% is matter of some other kind that has gravity but does not shine, absorb or interact with anything we have built. The remaining 68% is something that makes the expansion of space accelerate. Neither has ever been detected except through gravity. This chapter is about the evidence, which is strong, and the explanations, which are not settled.

On the plate: the universe’s energy budget.

Galaxies spin too fast

Stars far from a galaxy’s centre should orbit slowly, as planets far from the Sun do, if most of the mass is in the visible disk. In the 1970s Vera Rubin and Kent Ford measured stars and gas in spiral galaxies and found orbital speeds that stay flat far beyond the visible edge. Add a halo of invisible matter of mass 0.00 times the disk’s. More independent evidence

Gravitational lensing of background galaxies maps mass directly. In the Bullet Cluster, two galaxy clusters that passed through each other, the hot gas, most of the ordinary matter, was slowed and left in the middle, while the lensing mass passed through with the galaxies (Douglas Clowe and colleagues, 2006). The mass is not where the ordinary matter is. And the pattern of hot and cold spots in the cosmic microwave background, measured by Planck, fits only if non-baryonic matter is five times the baryonic: Ωbh2=0.0224\Omega_bh^2 = 0.0224Ωb​h2=0.0224, Ωch2=0.120\Omega_ch^2 = 0.120Ωc​h2=0.120. Modified-gravity theories without dark matter struggle to fit all of these at once. Established that something behaves as cold dark matter.

What could it be?

WIMPs, weakly interacting massive particles of roughly 10 GeV–10 TeV, would naturally leave the right abundance after the Big Bang. Detectors deep underground look for a nucleus recoiling from a WIMP collision. A 100 GeV WIMP moving at 230 km/s can kick a xenon nucleus with at most about 30 keV. Choose a WIMP mass, 100 GeV, to see the recoil spectrum. In 2025 LZ, with 4.2 tonne-years of xenon, found no signal and limited the spin-independent cross section to below 2.2×10−482.2\times10^{-48}2.2×10−48 cm² at 40 GeV.

Axions, very light particles originally proposed to explain why QCD conserves CP (Chapter 82’s θ), are searched for by converting them to microwave photons in a magnetic field. Other candidates include sterile neutrinos and primordial black holes. Speculative which, if any.

Dark energy

In 1998 two teams, led by Saul Perlmutter and by Brian Schmidt and Adam Riess, used exploding white dwarfs as standard candles and found that distant ones were fainter than expected: the expansion is accelerating. The simplest explanation is a cosmological constant Λ\LambdaΛ, an energy density of empty space. Chapter 60 showed why that is puzzling: field theory estimates the vacuum energy as 1012010^{120}10120 times too large.

Two current tensions. The DESI survey’s 2025 data, combined with supernovae and the CMB, prefer dark energy that weakens over time at 2.8 to 4.2 standard deviations depending on the combination; later analyses find less. And the expansion rate today, the Hubble constant, comes out at 73.2±0.973.2 \pm 0.973.2±0.9 km/s/Mpc from SH0ES’s Cepheid-calibrated supernovae but closer to 67–70 from the CMB and from other calibrations such as the tip of the red giant branch. Debated, both.
