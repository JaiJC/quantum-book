WKB approximation

In 1913 Bohr quantized the atom with a rule that seemed arbitrary: the action around an orbit must be a whole number of Planck’s constants. Thirteen years later, the Schrödinger equation replaced it. But Bohr’s rule was not wrong. It is the first term of an expansion of the Schrödinger equation in powers of ℏ\hbarℏ, and with one small correction it predicts energies to a fraction of a percent. This chapter derives it, and uses it to explain neutrons that bounce on a mirror in quantized steps of a few micrometres.

On the plate: an exact wavefunction, and the envelope the semiclassical method predicts for it.

Where it breaks

At a turning point, where E=VE = VE=V, p=0p = 0p=0 and the formula blows up. The wavelength is no longer short compared with the distance over which it changes. The cure: near the turning point, the potential is nearly a straight line, and the Schrödinger equation for a linear potential is solved exactly by the Airy function. Matching the Airy function to WKB on both sides gives the connection formulas: the oscillating wave inside joins the decaying one outside with a phase of π/4\pi/4π/4.

Neutrons on a mirror

A particle above a hard floor in gravity has the potential mgzmgzmgz above z=0z = 0z=0 and a wall below. The exact levels involve the zeros of the Airy function; WKB, with a wall on one side, gives (n−14)(n - \tfrac14)(n−41​) instead of (n+12)(n + \tfrac12)(n+21​) and matches them to within 1% even for the lowest.

For neutrons, the natural length is 5.87 µm, and the lowest states have heights of about 13.7, 24.0, 32.4 and 39.8 µm. In 2002 Valery Nesvizhevsky and colleagues at the Institut Laue–Langevin sent ultracold neutrons over a mirror under an absorbing ceiling. Lowering the ceiling below about 15 µm stopped transmission almost completely: the neutrons could not fit. Gravity, the weakest force, quantized in the lab. Established

This is Gamow’s factor for alpha decay from Chapter 34. Applied to a Coulomb barrier, it gives the Geiger–Nuttall law. The same formula sets the rates of fusion in the Sun and of hydrogen transfer in some enzymes, which Part Four will examine.

When to trust it

The condition is that the wavelength changes little over one wavelength: ∣dλ/dx∣≪1|d\lambda/dx| \ll 1∣dλ/dx∣≪1. That fails near turning points, at sharp steps, and for low quantum numbers. Michael Berry and Kenneth Mount showed in 1972 that the full series in ℏ\hbarℏ is asymptotic, like the perturbation series of Chapter 44, and that its failures encode real physics, such as the exponentially small reflection from a smooth step that no finite order captures.
