Quantum entropy, trace distance, fidelity

How different are two quantum states? There is a precise answer, and it is operational: exactly as different as your best chance of telling them apart in a single shot. The trace distance captures it. A second measure, fidelity, captures how close they are, and an entropy captures how mixed each one is. These three quantities run through every later chapter on error correction, benchmarking and cryptography.

On the plate: two states in the Bloch ball, with their distance, fidelity and the best odds of distinguishing them.

Trace distance

Define D(ρ,σ)=12∥ρ−σ∥1D(\rho, \sigma) = \tfrac12\|\rho - \sigma\|_1D(ρ,σ)=21​∥ρ−σ∥1​, half the sum of the absolute eigenvalues of the difference. For qubits it is half the Euclidean distance between Bloch vectors. Its meaning: the best single-shot probability of telling them apart, given each with probability one half, is 12(1+D)\tfrac12(1 + D)21​(1+D), the Helstrom bound of Chapter 101 in general form. Move the states: lengths 1.00 and 0.80, angle between them 60°. Trace distance can only shrink under a channel: noise makes states harder to tell apart, never easier.
