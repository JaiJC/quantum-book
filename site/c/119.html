Quantum signal processing and QSVT

Grover’s search, Hamiltonian simulation, matrix inversion and phase estimation were discovered separately, over twenty years. In 2019 András Gilyén, Yuan Su, Guang Hao Low and Nathan Wiebe showed they are all one algorithm with different settings: choose a polynomial, and a sequence of simple phase rotations applies that polynomial to the singular values of a matrix. The framework, the quantum singular value transformation, has become the common language of quantum algorithms.

Block encodings and QSVT

To act on a matrix AAA rather than a number, embed it in the top-left block of a larger unitary, a block encoding, U=(A/α⋅⋅⋅)U = \begin{pmatrix}A/\alpha & \cdot\\ \cdot & \cdot\end{pmatrix}U=(A/α⋅​⋅⋅​), built for instance by linear combinations of unitaries (Chapter 118). Alternating UUU, U†U^\daggerU† and phase rotations on the ancilla then applies PPP to every singular value of A/αA/\alphaA/α at once. That is the QSVT. Its special cases: PPP close to a step function gives search and amplitude amplification; P(x)≈e−ixtP(x) \approx e^{-ixt}P(x)≈e−ixt gives Hamiltonian simulation with optimal cost; P(x)≈1/xP(x) \approx 1/xP(x)≈1/x on the relevant range gives matrix inversion (Chapter 120); and phase estimation can be phrased the same way. John Martyn and colleagues called it a “grand unification” in 2021.
