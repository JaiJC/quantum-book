Symmetry and group theory basics

Turn a cup once around and it is back where it started. Turn an electron once around, a full 360°, and its quantum state comes back with a minus sign. Only after a second full turn, 720° in all, is it truly back. This is not a quirk of the mathematics: neutron experiments in 1975 measured the sign flip directly. Established

To see how a rotation can need two turns, we need the language of symmetry: group theory. It also explains why conservation laws exist, why molecules have handedness, and, in Part Two, why the forces of nature have the shapes they do.

What a group is

Take an equilateral triangle and list every move that leaves it looking the same: do nothing, rotate by 120° or 240°, or flip it across one of its three axes. Six moves. Doing one move then another is again one of the six. Every move can be undone. There is a do-nothing move. And grouping doesn’t matter: (A then B) then C equals A then (B then C). Those four facts are the definition of a group.

Order matters

Rotate then flip, and compare with flip then rotate. The results differ: the group is non-commutative. The plate’s table shows every product, with the row move done first. That a rotation and a reflection do not commute is the same kind of fact as {Lx,Ly}=Lz\{L_x, L_y\} = L_z{Lx​,Ly​}=Lz​ in Chapter 13: rotations about different axes interfere with each other.

Representations: groups as matrices

A representation assigns a matrix to each move so that multiplying matrices matches combining moves. The triangle’s moves can be written as 2×2 rotation and reflection matrices acting on the plane. The same group can have many representations, and in quantum mechanics the states of a system form representations of its symmetry group. Energy levels that must be degenerate, such as the three ppp orbitals of an atom, are degenerate because they carry one representation together.

Smooth symmetries and their generators

Rotations in space form a continuous group, SO(3). Any rotation can be built from many tiny ones, and a tiny rotation by angle ϵ\epsilonϵ about an axis is almost the identity: R≈1−iϵJR \approx 1 - i\epsilon JR≈1−iϵJ. The three matrices Jx,Jy,JzJ_x, J_y, J_zJx​,Jy​,Jz​ are the generators, and they satisfy

This is a Lie algebra, and it is the quantum version of the angular-momentum brackets. In quantum mechanics the generator of rotations is angular momentum (divided by ℏ\hbarℏ), just as the generator of translations is momentum and the generator of time shifts is energy. Noether’s theorem, seen from here, says the generator of a symmetry is conserved.

You can feel the same topology with a belt: fix one end, attach the other to a book, and turn the book once. The belt has a twist you cannot remove without turning the book back. Turn it twice, and the twist can be undone by looping the belt around the book. This is Dirac’s belt trick.

Mirror images

Some symmetries cannot be done by moving an object at all. A left hand and a right hand are mirror images, but no rotation turns one into the other. Molecules can be chiral in the same way, and life is strikingly one-handed: the amino acids in your proteins are almost all of the left-handed form, and the sugars of DNA are right-handed. of the molecule on the plate and try to rotate it back. You cannot.

The weak nuclear force, alone among the forces, also tells left from right, as you will see in Part Two.
