Quantum-enhanced imaging and spectroscopy for medicine

Light damages living cells. Push more photons into a microscope to beat shot noise and you start cooking the sample. Quantum light offers a way around that trade-off: squeezed light has less noise than the shot-noise limit in one quadrature, so it buys signal-to-noise without adding photons. In 2021 a team in Brisbane used it to image molecular vibrations inside living cells more gently. Other quantum-spectroscopy claims fared worse under scrutiny. This chapter separates the two.

Shot noise and squeezing

With coherent laser light, photon counts fluctuate as N\sqrt NN​, so signal-to-noise grows only as the square root of power: the standard quantum limit (Chapter 154). Squeezed light redistributes quantum uncertainty, reducing amplitude noise below shot noise at the cost of more phase noise. Gentler microscopy

In stimulated Raman microscopy, two lasers excite molecular vibrations, mapping chemicals inside cells without labels. Catxere Casacio, Warwick Bowen and colleagues injected squeezed light and reported in Nature in 2021 a 35% improvement in signal-to-noise, at powers where the classical limit is set by photodamage. Set the squeezing to 2.6 dB. The plate shows signal-to-noise rising with power until the damage threshold; squeezing lifts the whole curve, so the same image quality comes at lower dose. Related ideas, ghost imaging and imaging with undetected photons (Gabriela Barreto Lemos and colleagues, 2014), image with light that never touches the detector, useful at wavelengths where detectors are poor. Established, modest gains.

The ETPA controversy

Entangled photon pairs can in principle be absorbed together by a molecule much more efficiently than independent photons at low flux, which promised two-photon fluorescence microscopy at tiny, harmless intensities. Several groups reported large “entangled two-photon absorption” cross-sections, for example Dmitri Tabakaev and colleagues in 2021. Careful follow-ups then found the effects absent or far smaller: Tiemo Landes and colleagues set upper bounds orders of magnitude below the claims in 2021, Kristen Parzuchowski and colleagues found null results the same year, and Alexander Mikhaylov and colleagues showed in 2022 that ordinary one-photon “hot-band” absorption and fluorescence can mimic the signal. The large-enhancement claims have not survived. It is a useful lesson in controls (Chapter 180). Debated, large ETPA enhancement effectively refuted.

What you now have

Shot noise and squeezed light, the decibel arithmetic of squeezing, the 2021 squeezed Raman microscopy result, imaging with undetected photons, and the rise and fall of large entangled two-photon absorption claims.
