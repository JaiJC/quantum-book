Quantum sensing and metrology

The most mature quantum technology is not computing. It is measurement. Atomic clocks keep time to a second in the age of the universe; magnetometers built from defects in diamond have detected the magnetic signal of a single neuron firing; and the LIGO detectors, which heard black holes merging a billion light-years away, now inject specially prepared “squeezed” vacuum light to listen more sharply. The same fragility that makes qubits hard to protect makes them exquisite sensors.

On the plate: how precision improves with the number of probes.

Ramsey interferometry

Put a qubit into superposition with a π/2\pi/2π/2 pulse, let it evolve for time TTT so that a frequency difference δ\deltaδ accumulates a phase φ=δT\varphi = \delta Tφ=δT, then apply a second π/2\pi/2π/2 pulse and measure. The probability of finding the qubit excited is 12(1+cos⁡δT)\tfrac12(1+\cos\delta T)21​(1+cosδT): a fringe whose spacing narrows as TTT grows, until decoherence (Chapter 135) washes it out. Interrogation time 1.0 (in units of the coherence time). Norman Ramsey’s method, which earned the 1989 Nobel Prize, underlies every atomic clock. Established

Standard quantum limit and Heisenberg limit

With NNN independent atoms, the phase uncertainty falls as 1/N1/\sqrt N1/N​: the standard quantum limit (SQL), just like averaging coin flips. Entangling the atoms, for example in a GHZ state where the phase accumulates NNN times faster, can reach 1/N1/N1/N: the Heisenberg limit. The quantum Cramér–Rao bound Δφ≥1/νFQ\Delta\varphi\ge1/\sqrt{\nu F_Q}Δφ≥1/νFQ​​, with FQF_QFQ​ the quantum Fisher information and ν\nuν the number of repetitions, sets the ultimate precision for any state and measurement; FQ=NF_Q = NFQ​=N for independent atoms and up to N2N^2N2 for entangled ones. Vittorio Giovannetti, Seth Lloyd and Lorenzo Maccone laid out the framework in 2004. Choose N=10xN = 10^xN=10x with x=x =x= 6.0. Entangled states are fragile, so in practice gains come from squeezing: redistributing uncertainty between two quadratures. LIGO’s frequency-dependent squeezing, reported by Dhruva Ganapathy and colleagues in 2023, cut quantum noise by up to 5.8 dB and extended the detection range by 15–18%. Established

What you now have

Ramsey interferometry, the standard quantum limit and Heisenberg limit, the quantum Cramér–Rao bound and Fisher information, squeezing and LIGO, and NV-centre magnetometry by ODMR.

Three questions to carry with you.

Why does independent averaging give 1/√N?tap
Independent errors add in quadrature, as for coin flips.
How does entanglement reach 1/N?tap
An N-atom GHZ state accumulates phase N times faster, raising the Fisher information to N².
Why are NV centres useful in biology?tap
They read magnetic fields optically at room temperature, at nanometre to micrometre scales.
