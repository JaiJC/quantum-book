General relativity and quantum gravity: string theory, loop gravity, open problems

General relativity describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime, and it has passed every test: the bending of starlight, the timing of binary pulsars, the ripples of colliding black holes picked up by LIGO in 2015, the shadow of a black hole photographed in 2019. Quantum field theory describes everything else, equally well. Inside a black hole, or at the first instant of the Big Bang, both should apply at once, and they disagree. This last chapter of Part Two is about that disagreement, what is known for sure, and the candidate theories that try to resolve it.

On the plate: orbits around a black hole. Close in, Newton’s ellipses stop closing, and below a certain radius no stable orbit exists.

Why gravity resists quantization

That does not make it useless: as an effective field theory (Chapter 70), quantum general relativity gives reliable, tiny corrections below the Planck scale, such as the quantum correction to Newton’s potential. Black holes are hot

In 1974 Stephen Hawking applied quantum field theory near a horizon and found that a black hole emits thermal radiation at temperature

Hawking radiation has never been observed from an astrophysical black hole; analogues in fluids and optics show the same mathematics. Established as theory; unobserved directly.

The information paradox

If a black hole formed from a pure quantum state evaporates into exactly thermal radiation, the final state is mixed: information is lost, contradicting unitarity (Chapter 31). Don Page showed in 1993 that if evaporation is unitary, the entanglement entropy of the radiation must rise and then fall, the Page curve, turning over halfway through. In 2019 calculations using semiclassical gravity with new saddle points, “islands”, reproduced the Page curve. Whether that resolves the paradox, and what it says about the interior, is debated. Speculative

Candidate theories

String theory replaces point particles with strings; one vibration is a graviton, and its loops are finite. It has produced deep results, such as the counting of black-hole entropy for special black holes and the AdS/CFT correspondence (Juan Maldacena, 1997), in which a gravitational theory in a curved space is exactly equivalent to a non-gravitational field theory on its boundary. It has made no confirmed prediction about our universe. Loop quantum gravity quantizes geometry directly, giving discrete areas and volumes, but has difficulty recovering smooth spacetime and has no confirmed predictions either. Other approaches, asymptotic safety, causal sets and more, each have advocates. Speculative, all of them.
