Asymptotic freedom and confinement

Hit a proton hard enough and the quarks inside behave as if they were almost free. Try to pull one out, and the force between it and the rest never weakens: it stays at about 15 tonnes, however far you pull, until the energy stored in the stretched field snaps into a new quark–antiquark pair. You never get a single quark, only more hadrons. The first half of that story is proven and won the 2004 Nobel Prize. Proving the second half is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems, with a million dollars attached.

On the plate: two quarks joined by a tube of gluon field. Pull them apart.

Antiscreening

In QED, virtual pairs screen charge, and α\alphaα grows at short distance (Chapter 70). In QCD, quark loops screen too, but gluon loops, possible because gluons carry colour (Chapter 75), do the opposite and win. At one loop,

Confinement

At large distance the gluon field between a quark and an antiquark does not spread like electric field lines; it squeezes into a narrow tube with constant energy per length, the string tension σ≈0.18\sigma \approx 0.18σ≈0.18 GeV², about 0.9 GeV per fermi, a force near 15 tonnes. Stretch the tube by 0.60 fm. Past about 1.2 fm the stored energy exceeds what it takes to make a light quark pair, and the tube breaks into two mesons. This is why high-energy quarks emerge as jets of hadrons.

The evidence is strong: computer simulations of QCD on a spacetime lattice (Chapter 74) show the linear potential, and compute the proton’s mass from first principles to within a few percent. But no one has proven mathematically that Yang–Mills theory has a mass gap, a lightest particle with nonzero mass, which is what confinement requires. That is the Clay Institute’s Millennium Problem. Established in practice; Debated only in the sense of being unproven.

Where your mass comes from

The proton’s mass, almost all of it, is the energy of confined quarks and gluons (Chapter 77). Without quark masses at all, QCD would still produce protons with nearly the same mass, set by ΛQCD\Lambda_{\text{QCD}}ΛQCD​, a scale that appears from the running of a dimensionless coupling: dimensional transmutation. A theory with no mass scale generates one, and that scale makes up most of the visible universe.
