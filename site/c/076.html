Spontaneous symmetry breaking

Balance a pencil on its tip. The situation is perfectly symmetric: no direction is special. But the pencil cannot stay; it falls, and when it does it points one way. The laws were symmetric; the outcome is not. A magnet below its Curie temperature does the same: the equations have no preferred direction, but the magnetization picks one. When this happens in a field theory, the symmetry is said to be spontaneously broken, and its fingerprints are massless particles, or, as for the pions, nearly massless ones.

On the plate: the “Mexican hat” potential. A ball at the top is symmetric and unstable; in the brim it has chosen a direction.

The Mexican hat

The vacuum must sit somewhere on that circle, and wherever it sits, it breaks the symmetry. The theory is still symmetric: rotating the chosen vacuum gives another equally good vacuum. Established

Two kinds of ripple

Expand around the chosen vacuum. Push the field : it rolls back and oscillates, a massive particle with m2=2μ2m^2 = 2\mu^2m2=2μ2. Push it : it costs no energy and nothing pushes it back, a massless particle. That flat direction is a Goldstone boson.

Jeffrey Goldstone’s theorem (1961, proved generally with Abdus Salam and Steven Weinberg in 1962): every continuous global symmetry that is spontaneously broken gives one massless spin-0 particle for each broken generator. Breaking O(N) rotations of NNN real fields down to O(N − 1) gives N−1N - 1N−1 Goldstones.

Where you meet them

In a ferromagnet, the broken symmetry is spin rotation, and the Goldstone modes are spin waves, magnons, which cost almost no energy at long wavelength. In a crystal, broken translations give sound waves, phonons. In a superfluid, the broken phase symmetry gives the sound mode of helium-4. These are all examples where the theorem, carried over from field theory to materials, predicts gapless excitations.

Pions

Yoichiro Nambu proposed in 1960, by analogy with superconductivity, that the strong force spontaneously breaks a symmetry of nearly massless quarks, chiral symmetry, which rotates left- and right-handed quarks separately. Three generators are broken, so there should be three Goldstone bosons: the three pions. Pions are not exactly massless because the up and down quarks have small masses that break the symmetry slightly to begin with, but at 140 MeV they are far lighter than the proton at 938 MeV. Their mass squared grows in proportion to the quark masses, as lattice QCD confirms. Nambu shared the 2008 Nobel Prize. Established

What you now have

The Mexican-hat potential and its circle of vacua, radial (massive) and angular (massless) excitations, Goldstone’s theorem and its counting, Goldstone modes in magnets, crystals and superfluids, and pions as pseudo-Goldstone bosons of chiral symmetry.

Three questions to carry with you.

Is a spontaneously broken theory still symmetric?tap
The laws are; the vacuum isn’t. Symmetry maps one vacuum to another equally valid one.
Why is a Goldstone boson massless?tap
Moving along the circle of vacua costs no energy.
Why are pions light but not massless?tap
They are Goldstones of chiral symmetry, which small quark masses already break slightly.
