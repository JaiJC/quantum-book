Electromagnetism II: light as a wave

Put Maxwell’s equations in empty space, with no charges anywhere, and they still have something to say. A changing magnetic field makes an electric field; a changing electric field, thanks to Maxwell’s extra term, makes a magnetic field. Each keeps the other going, and the pair can travel on their own. Maxwell worked out how fast, and got the speed of light.

This chapter shows how light falls out of the equations, what it carries, and then how the same equations predict that every atom should destroy itself in a hundred-billionth of a second. That failure is where quantum physics begins.

What a light wave looks like

In a plane wave, E\mathbf EE and B\mathbf BB oscillate in step, at right angles to each other and to the direction of travel. The plate shows them in perspective; to see that the two fields stay perpendicular. Their sizes are tied: B=E/cB = E/cB=E/c.

Heinrich Hertz made and detected such waves with sparks and wire loops in 1887–88, at radio frequencies, and measured their speed. The visible light you read by is the same thing with a wavelength a million times shorter.

Light carries energy and push

The energy flowing through each square metre per second is the Poynting vector, S=E×B/μ0\mathbf S = \mathbf E\times\mathbf B/\mu_0S=E×B/μ0​. Sunlight at Earth delivers about 1361 W per square metre, which means an electric field swinging with an amplitude of about 1000 V/m.

Light also carries momentum: absorbing it pushes with a pressure S/cS/cS/c, and reflecting it twice that. For sunlight on a mirror that is 9 micropascals, a force of 9 newtons on a square kilometre. Tiny, but in space it never stops, and solar sails such as JAXA’s IKAROS (2010) have flown on it. Established

Shaking a charge makes light

A charge moving steadily carries its field along. A charge that accelerates sends out a ripple. The power radiated is the Larmor formula:

A charge oscillating up and down radiates most strongly sideways and not at all along its line of motion, the doughnut pattern on the plate. Every radio antenna has this pattern.

The atom that should not exist

Now the crisis. In the planetary picture of the atom, an electron circles the nucleus. Circling is accelerating, so the electron must radiate, lose energy and spiral inward. Use the Larmor formula and you can work out how long the fall takes, from the size of a hydrogen atom down to the nucleus:

Light as an electromagnetic wave with speed 1/μ0ε01/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}1/μ0​ε0​​, energy flow and radiation pressure, radiation from accelerating charges, gauge freedom, and a clear failure of classical physics.

Three questions to carry with you.

Why does a classical orbiting electron radiate?tap
Moving in a circle means accelerating, and accelerating charges radiate (Larmor).
How were E and B oriented in a light wave?tap
Perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel, oscillating in step, with B = E/c.
What does a gauge transformation change?tap
The potentials φ and A, but not the fields E and B, so nothing measurable.
