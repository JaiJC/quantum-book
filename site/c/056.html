The Klein–Gordon equation

In the winter of 1925, before he found the equation that bears his name, Schrödinger wrote down a relativistic one. He applied it to hydrogen, got the fine structure wrong, and put it aside. Oskar Klein and Walter Gordon published it in 1926, and for a decade it seemed a dead end: its probability could go negative. It came back as the equation of every particle with no spin. The Higgs boson obeys it.

Mix in negative frequencies: 0.30. The plate shows ρ\rhoρ: amber where positive, red where negative. The two parts move apart, and ρ\rhoρ cannot be a probability. In 1934 Wolfgang Pauli and Victor Weisskopf reinterpreted it as charge density: the positive parts are particles, the negative parts antiparticles of opposite charge.

Negative energy, read correctly

A negative-frequency solution moving forward in time, e+iEte^{+iEt}e+iEt, has the same form as a positive-energy one moving backward. Ernst Stückelberg and later Feynman read it that way: a negative-energy particle going into the past is an antiparticle with positive energy going into the future. Nothing ever has negative energy. In the field theory of Chapter 60, the negative-frequency part of ϕ\phiϕ becomes the operator that creates antiparticles, and the energy of every state is positive.

Hideki Yukawa proposed in 1935 that the force holding nuclei together comes from exchanging a particle of mass about 100–200 MeV, to give a range of about 1 fm. The pion, found in 1947 at 139.6 MeV, fits: 1/m=1.411/m = 1.411/m=1.41 fm. Set the mediator mass to 140 MeV; zero mass gives back Coulomb’s law. Heavy mediators give short-range forces: the W and Z, at 80–91 GeV, reach only about 0.002 fm, which is why the weak force is weak. Established

Where it lives now

As a single-particle wave equation, Klein–Gordon fails, for the reasons of Chapter 55. As a field equation it is exactly right for spin-0 particles: pions in effective theories, and the Higgs field, whose excitation was discovered in 2012 at 125 GeV. Schrödinger’s discarded equation turned out to be the first piece of the Standard Model.
