Derive-from-scratch exam: quantum mechanics

Understanding quantum mechanics means being able to derive its key results with a blank page. This capstone is an exam: twelve derivations, no notes, about three hours. Each is something the book derived, checked by code, and built on. Do them in order or pick at random; the plate holds the final results so you can check yourself, and the rubric awards partial credit for method, because a correct method with an algebra slip shows more understanding than a remembered answer.

On the plate: the questions and their checked results.

Rules and rubric

Work with pen and paper; no notes, no software. For each question score yourself out of 10: setup (3), stating the Hamiltonian, boundary conditions or assumptions; method (4), the right technique carried through; result (2), the correct final expression; check (1), a units, limit or sanity check. A score of 80 or more out of 120 means you own the core of Part 1. Below 60, revisit the chapters named with each question. Established as method.

The twelve

Question 1; . Derive: (1) the hydrogen ground-state energy; (2) the oscillator spectrum from ladder operators; (3) the infinite-well spectrum; (4) the minimum-uncertainty product for a Gaussian; (5) spin-½ precession in a field; (6) the singlet and triplet from two spin-½; (7) the CHSH bound and its quantum maximum; (8) Fermi’s golden rule; (9) a variational bound for helium with an effective charge; (10) the WKB tunnelling exponent; (11) the free-particle propagator from the path integral; (12) the Rutherford cross-section in the Born approximation. Established

After the exam

For every question below 7, reread its chapter and redo the derivation the next day without looking. Then explain one derivation aloud to someone else, or write it as if for a student: teaching exposes the steps you skipped. The same approach works for the QFT exam that follows. Established as method.

What you now have

A twelve-question derivation exam spanning Part 1, a partial-credit rubric, checked final results, and a method for turning weak answers into strong ones.
