Single-qubit gates and rotations

Every operation you can perform on a single qubit, every gate in every quantum algorithm acting on one qubit at a time, is a rotation of the Bloch sphere. Nothing more. The Pauli X gate is a half turn about the x-axis; the Hadamard is a half turn about the diagonal between x and z; the T gate is an eighth of a turn about z. Learn to see gates as rotations and single-qubit circuits become geometry you can do in your head.

Why these gates

H, S and the two-qubit CNOT generate the Clifford gates, which turn out to be easy to simulate classically (Chapter 108). Adding T, a rotation by an angle that is not a multiple of 90°, makes the set universal: any rotation can be approximated arbitrarily well by sequences of H and T (Chapter 100). In fault-tolerant machines, T gates are the expensive ones, needing “magic states” (Chapter 140), so algorithms are often costed by their T count.
