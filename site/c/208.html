Reading and critiquing a research paper

The capstones are where you stop reading and start doing. The first skill is the one every researcher uses daily: reading a paper critically. Not hunting for flaws, but separating what was claimed from what was shown, checking that the controls support the conclusion, and noticing where the abstract or press release reaches further than the data. This chapter gives you a referee’s rubric and walks it through two real papers from this book.

The rubric

For any paper, write down: (1) the claim in one sentence, in the authors’ strongest form; (2) the evidence offered for each part; (3) the controls: what alternative explanations were excluded, and how; (4) the statistics: sample sizes, error bars, whether the analysis was fixed in advance; (5) the baseline: what the result is compared against, and whether it is the strongest available; (6) the overreach: where the title, abstract or discussion claims more than the results support; and (7) the next experiment that would settle what remains open. A referee report is these seven items, written politely and specifically. Established as method.
