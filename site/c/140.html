Fault tolerance: threshold theorem, magic state distillation, lattice surgery

Error correction is not enough on its own. The circuits that measure syndromes are made of the same faulty gates as everything else, and a gate applied to a code block could spread one error into several. Fault tolerance is the discipline of computing on encoded data so that errors never cascade. Its crowning result, proved independently by several groups in 1996–97, says that if every component fails less often than some threshold, arbitrarily long quantum computations are possible with only polylogarithmic overhead. The heart of the proof is one inequality.

On the plate: error rates through levels of encoding.

Magic states

By Eastin–Knill (Chapter 138), the surface code’s easy gates are Clifford gates, which a classical computer can simulate (Chapter 108). Universality needs a T gate, applied by consuming a magic state ∣T⟩=(∣0⟩+eiπ/4∣1⟩)/2|T\rangle = (|0\rangle + e^{i\pi/4}|1\rangle)/\sqrt2∣T⟩=(∣0⟩+eiπ/4∣1⟩)/2​ through a short teleportation circuit. Magic states prepared directly are noisy. Sergey Bravyi and Kitaev showed in 2005 how to distil them: 15 noisy copies go in, and the 15-qubit Reed–Muller code’s checks reject bad batches, so one cleaner copy comes out with error ≈35p3\approx35p^3≈35p3. Input error -3.0 (log₁₀). Two rounds take 10⁻³ to below 10⁻²⁰. Distillation factories once dominated every resource estimate. In 2024 Craig Gidney, Noah Shutty and Cody Jones proposed magic-state cultivation: grow a magic state inside a small code, checking it repeatedly, reaching about 2 × 10⁻⁹ error at 10⁻³ physical noise for roughly the cost of a CNOT. It has been benchmarked in simulation for state preparation; its place in full computations is still being worked out. Established for distillation; Debated for cultivation’s full role.

Lattice surgery

Surface-code patches cannot easily apply a transversal CNOT to each other on a flat chip. Dominic Horsman, Austin Fowler, Simon Devitt and Rodney Van Meter introduced lattice surgery in 2012: measure joint operators such as ZAZBZ_AZ_BZA​ZB​ by temporarily merging two patches along a boundary for ddd rounds, then splitting them. A CNOT becomes a sequence of joint measurements plus a classical correction. Daniel Litinski’s 2019 “A Game of Surface Codes” turned layout into a board game of tiles and time steps, and showed that a whole algorithm can be compiled into Pauli-product measurements and magic-state consumption. Why are magic states needed?tap
