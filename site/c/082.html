Part Two · Quantum fields and particles

The full Standard Model

Everything you have learned in Part Two now fits on a single page: the Lagrangian of the Standard Model. A few lines describe every particle ever found and every interaction except gravity, and they have survived every test at every collider for fifty years. Its ingredients are a gauge group, a list of fields with their charges, and nineteen numbers that must be measured. This chapter writes it down, checks a hidden consistency condition that the charges must satisfy, and counts the numbers.

On the plate: one generation of matter, with its charges under the three forces.

Anomalies must cancel

A symmetry of the classical Lagrangian can fail in the quantum theory, through triangle diagrams with chiral fermions running in the loop: an anomaly. For a gauge symmetry this would be fatal, destroying the redundancy that removes unphysical states (Chapter 74). The conditions for cancellation are sums over all left-handed fermions in a generation (right-handed fields counted as left-handed antiparticles): ∑Y=0\sum Y = 0∑Y=0, ∑Y3=0\sum Y^3 = 0∑Y3=0, and two mixed sums.

Change the quark doublet’s hypercharge to 0.167, adjusting uRu_RuR​ and dRd_RdR​ to keep the quark charges consistent with the Higgs couplings. The plate computes all four anomaly sums. Only the observed value, 16\tfrac1661​, makes them all vanish: the quark charges are tied to the lepton charges, and quarks must come in three colours for it to work. Within each generation, quarks and leptons need each other. Established

Nineteen numbers

The Standard Model has nineteen free parameters, fixed only by experiment: six quark masses, three charged-lepton masses, four parameters of quark mixing (three angles and a phase, Chapter 83), three gauge couplings, two for the Higgs (its mass and vacuum value), and the strong-CP angle θQCD\theta_{\text{QCD}}θQCD​, measured to be below about 10−1010^{-10}10−10. Neutrino masses and mixings, discovered after the Standard Model was written (Chapter 85), add seven to nine more. The plate lays them out. The masses alone span a factor of about 340,000 from the electron to the top quark, and about 101210^{12}1012 once the neutrinos are included, with no known reason.

What you now have

The whole Standard Model Lagrangian, its gauge group and field content with hypercharges, Yukawa couplings, gauge anomaly cancellation and why it links quarks to leptons, and the count of its nineteen parameters.
