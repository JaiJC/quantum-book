Special relativity

Cosmic rays strike the top of the atmosphere and make muons, heavy cousins of the electron that live, on average, 2.2 millionths of a second before decaying. Even at nearly the speed of light, that is time to cover only about 660 metres. Yet muons rain down on sea level by the thousand, after a journey of tens of kilometres.

They survive because their clocks run slow. This chapter shows why, starting from two simple statements, and ends at the formula that lets CERN make new particles out of energy.

Two postulates and a light clock

Einstein started in 1905 from two claims. The laws of physics are the same for everyone moving steadily. And the speed of light is the same for all of them, whatever the motion of the source. Michelson and Morley had already looked in 1887 for a change in light’s speed as the Earth moves, and found none. Established

Build a clock from a pulse of light bouncing between two mirrors. Moving at 0.60 of the speed of light, its pulse must travel along a longer diagonal path, as seen from outside. Light’s speed is fixed, so each tick takes longer. The stretch factor is

The muons again

In 1963 David Frisch and James Smith counted muons on the summit of Mount Washington, then at sea level, 1,910 metres lower, selecting muons moving at about 0.995 of the speed of light. The trip takes 6.4 microseconds of our time, nearly three muon lifetimes. Without time dilation only about 1 in 18 should survive. They found about three-quarters, just what a slowdown factor near 10 predicts. Established

Set the muon’s speed to 0.995: the fraction surviving the 1,910-metre drop is 75%, against 5% if their clocks did not slow.

Spacetime diagrams

Events on a horizontal line happen at the same time for you. Events on the rocket’s tilted line happen at the same time for the rocket. They are different events. Two observers moving relative to each other disagree about which distant events are simultaneous: the relativity of simultaneity, the idea that unlocks every paradox in this subject.

Clocks on aeroplanes

In 1971 Joseph Hafele and Richard Keating flew four atomic clocks around the world on ordinary airliners, once eastward and once westward, and compared them with clocks left on the ground. Eastward, moving with the Earth’s spin, the flying clocks lost 59 ± 10 nanoseconds; westward they gained 273 ± 7. Theory, including the gravitational effect of altitude from general relativity, predicted −40 ± 23 and +275 ± 21. Established

Your phone’s GPS corrects for both effects every second; without the corrections its position would drift by about 10 kilometres a day.

The twin who travels

One twin stays home; the other flies to a star at high speed and back. Each sees the other’s clock running slow during the steady parts of the trip. Yet when they meet, the traveller is younger. The two are not equivalent: only the traveller changes direction, switching from one set of simultaneity lines to another at the turnaround. On a spacetime diagram the traveller’s bent path is simply shorter in elapsed time. There is no contradiction, only an unfamiliar geometry.

Energy, momentum and mass

Time and space mix under a boost; so do energy and momentum. They form a four-vector whose length every observer agrees on:

E2=(pc)2+(mc2)2E^2 = (pc)^2 + (mc^2)^2E2=(pc)2+(mc2)2

At rest, E=mc2E = mc^2E=mc2. For a collection of particles, the same combination of total energy and total momentum gives the invariant mass, which is how physicists recognize a Higgs boson from the particles it decays into.

It also says how much energy you need to make new particles. To create an antiproton by firing a proton at a proton at rest, p+p→p+p+p+pˉp + p \to p + p + p + \bar pp+p→p+p+p+pˉ​, the beam needs at least 6mpc2≈5.66m_pc^2 \approx 5.66mp​c2≈5.6 GeV of kinetic energy, far more than the 1.9 GeV of rest energy being created, because momentum must also be conserved. Berkeley’s Bevatron was built to reach about 6 GeV, and found the antiproton in 1955. Established

What breaks the symmetry between the twins?tap
Only the travelling twin turns around, changing inertial frame and with it the lines of simultaneity.
