Magnetic fields and the Zeeman effect

Wind a long, thin coil of wire and run a current through it. The magnetic field is all inside; outside, there is none at all. Send electrons around the outside, never through, and recombine them. Their interference pattern shifts as you change the current. The electrons respond to a magnetic field they never touch. Yakir Aharonov and David Bohm predicted this in 1959, and Akira Tonomura’s team confirmed it beyond doubt in 1986.

On the plate: two electron paths passing either side of a sealed magnetic region, and the fringes where they meet.

How a charge feels a magnetic field

In Chapter 15 the magnetic field was written as B=∇×A\mathbf B = \nabla\times\mathbf AB=∇×A, with A\mathbf AA the vector potential. Quantum mechanics brings in the field through A\mathbf AA, not B\mathbf BB, by the rule called minimal coupling:

H=(p−qA)22m+qϕH = \frac{(\mathbf p - q\mathbf A)^2}{2m} + q\phiH=2m(p−qA)2​+qϕ

A\mathbf AA is not unique: adding the gradient of any function χ\chiχ leaves B\mathbf BB unchanged. The Schrödinger equation stays the same if the wavefunction changes phase at the same time, ψ→eiqχ/ℏψ\psi \to e^{iq\chi/\hbar}\psiψ→eiqχ/ℏψ. This gauge invariance looks like bookkeeping here. In Part Five it becomes the principle from which the forces of the Standard Model are built. Established

Landau levels

A classical electron in a uniform field circles at the cyclotron frequency ωc=eB/m\omega_c = eB/mωc​=eB/m. Quantum mechanically, the motion in the plane is a harmonic oscillator (Chapter 35), so the energies are

En=ℏωc(n+12)E_n = \hbar\omega_c\left(n + \tfrac12\right)En​=ℏωc​(n+21​)

These are Landau levels. Each is enormously degenerate: one state for every flux quantum h/eh/eh/e passing through the sample, about 2.4×10142.4\times10^{14}2.4×1014 per square metre per tesla. Set the field to 10 T. The spacing grows with BBB and each level holds more states. When a two-dimensional sheet of electrons fills a whole number of these levels, its Hall resistance locks to h/ne2h/ne^2h/ne2 with astonishing precision: the quantum Hall effect, which Part Four takes up.

Splitting spectral lines

In 1896 Pieter Zeeman put a sodium flame between the poles of a magnet and saw its yellow lines broaden. A magnetic moment μ\boldsymbol\muμ in a field has energy −μ⋅B-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B−μ⋅B, so each level shifts by gJ mJ μBBg_J\,m_J\,\mu_B BgJ​mJ​μB​B, with the Bohr magneton μB=5.788×10−5\mu_B = 5.788\times10^{-5}μB​=5.788×10−5 eV/T and the Landé factors of Chapter 41.

Raise the field to 1.0 T. Because the upper and lower levels have different gJg_JgJ​, the D₁ line splits into four components and D₂ into six. This pattern was called anomalous, because the simple three-line splitting that classical theory predicted only appears for atoms with no net spin. Its explanation needed spin and gs=2g_s = 2gs​=2. When the field wins

The Zeeman formula assumes the field is weak compared with spin–orbit coupling, so L\mathbf LL and S\mathbf SS stay locked into J\mathbf JJ. In a strong field, they decouple and each precesses about B\mathbf BB separately, and the energies become μBB(mℓ+2ms)\mu_B B(m_\ell + 2m_s)μB​B(mℓ​+2ms​): the Paschen–Back regime.

A field it never touches

Back to the coil. Outside it, B=0\mathbf B = 0B=0 but A\mathbf AA is not zero; it circles the coil. An electron going round one side picks up a phase eℏ∫A⋅dl\frac{e}{\hbar}\int\mathbf A\cdot d\mathbf lℏe​∫A⋅dl, and the difference between the two sides is eΦ/ℏe\Phi/\hbareΦ/ℏ, set by the flux Φ\PhiΦ inside, which neither path enters.

Change the enclosed flux to 0.25 flux quanta h/eh/eh/e. The fringes slide by that fraction of a fringe. Tonomura’s group shielded a tiny ring magnet inside a superconductor, so no field could leak out and the electrons could not reach it, and still saw the shift. The vector potential, or more precisely the phase it produces around a loop, is physical. Established

What you now have

Minimal coupling and gauge invariance, Landau levels and their degeneracy, the Zeeman effect and the anomalous pattern, the Paschen–Back regime, and the Aharonov–Bohm effect.

Why does sodium’s D₂ line split into six?tap
The upper ²P₃/₂ level has four m_J values with g = 4/3, the lower ²S₁/₂ has two with g = 2; allowed jumps (Δm = 0, ±1) give six different energies.

One state per flux quantum h/e through the sample: eB/h per unit area.
What does the Aharonov–Bohm effect show?tap
That electrons respond to the vector potential’s loop phase eΦ/ħ even where B = 0.
