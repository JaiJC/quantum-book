Part Two · Quantum fields and particles

Wick's theorem

Every term in the Dyson series of Chapter 62 is the vacuum average of a product of many field operators. Computing such averages directly would be hopeless. In 1950 Gian-Carlo Wick found that they reduce to something a child could do: pair the fields up, two by two, in every possible way. Each pairing is one Feynman diagram, and each pair is one line.

On the plate: a row of fields joined in pairs. Step through every possible pairing.

Time ordering

Field operators at different times don’t commute, so the order matters. The S-matrix needs the time-ordered product, which simply puts later fields to the left:

T{ϕ(x)ϕ(y)}={ϕ(x)ϕ(y)x0>y0ϕ(y)ϕ(x)y0>x0T\{\phi(x)\phi(y)\} = \begin{cases}\phi(x)\phi(y) & x^0 > y^0\\ \phi(y)\phi(x) & y^0 > x^0\end{cases}T{ϕ(x)ϕ(y)}={ϕ(x)ϕ(y)ϕ(y)ϕ(x)​x0>y0y0>x0​

Its vacuum average defines the Feynman propagator, DF(x−y)=⟨0∣T{ϕ(x)ϕ(y)}∣0⟩D_F(x - y) = \langle0|T\{\phi(x)\phi(y)\}|0\rangleDF​(x−y)=⟨0∣T{ϕ(x)ϕ(y)}∣0⟩: the amplitude for a quantum created at the earlier point to be absorbed at the later one. Whichever comes first, the creation always happens before the absorption.

Pair everything

Wick’s theorem says the time-ordered product of free fields equals the sum, over all ways of pairing some of them, of the propagators for the pairs times normal-ordered products of the rest (Chapter 60). In the vacuum, normal-ordered pieces vanish, so

The propagator in momentum space

Fourier transformed, the propagator is strikingly simple:

DF(p)=ip2−m2+iϵD_F(p) = \frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}DF​(p)=p2−m2+iϵi​

It blows up when p2=m2p^2 = m^2p2=m2, the condition for a real particle. The tiny iϵi\epsiloniϵ decides how to go around the two poles at p0=±Ep^0 = \pm Ep0=±E when integrating over energy. The plate shows the complex p0p^0p0 plane: the +iϵ+i\epsilon+iϵ pushes the positive pole just below the axis and the negative one just above. Closing the contour below for later times picks up the positive-energy pole, a particle moving forward; closing above for earlier times picks up the negative one, an antiparticle moving forward the other way (Chapter 56). The iϵi\epsiloniϵ is the mathematical form of causality.

Symmetry factors

Pairings that give the same picture must be counted correctly. With one ϕ4/4!\phi^4/4!ϕ4/4! vertex and nothing else, the four fields at the same point pair in 3 ways, each joining the point to itself twice; the 1/4!1/4!1/4! and the 3 combine into a factor 3/24=1/83/24 = 1/83/24=1/8. That 1/81/81/8 is the diagram’s symmetry factor, one over the number of ways to rearrange its lines without changing it. The 1/4!1/4!1/4! in the Lagrangian is chosen precisely so that most diagrams come out with factor one.

What you now have

Time ordering, the Feynman propagator, Wick’s theorem as complete pairings with (n−1)!!(n-1)!!(n−1)!! terms, the momentum-space propagator and the iϵi\epsiloniϵ prescription, and symmetry factors.
