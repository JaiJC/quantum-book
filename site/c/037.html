Central potentials

The dumbbell of a p orbital, the cloverleaf of a d orbital, the round s: chemistry students learn these shapes as facts to memorize. They are not separate facts. Every one of them follows from a single property of the atom, that the force on the electron points straight at the nucleus and is the same in every direction. This chapter derives the shapes from that symmetry alone.

On the plate, the angular shapes turn slowly in space. Amber and red mark the two signs of the wavefunction.

Counting states

Since the energy depends on ℓ\ellℓ but not on mmm, each level is at least (2ℓ+1)(2\ell + 1)(2ℓ+1)-fold degenerate: rotating a state cannot change its energy when the potential is round. The plate draws them. Each level holds several ℓ\ellℓ values at once. Extra degeneracy like this always signals a hidden symmetry beyond rotation; hydrogen, next chapter, has one too.
