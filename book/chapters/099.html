Take two qubits, both 0. Apply a Hadamard to the first and a CNOT from the first to the second. Two gates. The result is a state in which neither qubit has any definite value, yet they always agree when measured the same way, and agree in a pattern that no pair of classical objects with pre-set answers can reproduce (Chapter 51). This is the Bell state, and it is the basic unit of entanglement: one ebit.

On the plate: two qubits and their correlations when measured at chosen angles.

The Bell basis

Four maximally entangled two-qubit states form a basis:

{{texd:|\Phi^\pm\rangle = \frac{|00\rangle \pm |11\rangle}{\sqrt2}, \qquad |\Psi^\pm\rangle = \frac{|01\rangle \pm |10\rangle}{\sqrt2}}}

Each is made from a computational basis state by H on the first qubit then CNOT: {{tex:|00\rangle\to|\Phi^+\rangle}}, {{tex:|01\rangle\to|\Psi^+\rangle}}, {{tex:|10\rangle\to|\Phi^-\rangle}}, {{tex:|11\rangle\to|\Psi^-\rangle}}. Running the circuit backwards, CNOT then H, turns a Bell state into two classical bits: a Bell measurement, the key step in teleportation (Chapter 103). Each qubit alone is maximally mixed, entropy 1 bit (Chapter 49). Established

Correlations at angles

Measure qubit A along an angle {{tex:a}} and B along {{tex:b}} in the x–z plane, by rotating each before measuring in z. For {{tex:|\Phi^+\rangle}} the correlation is {{tex:E(a, b) = \cos(a - b)}}. Set {{tex:a =}} 0° and {{tex:b =}} 45°. The plate computes the outcome probabilities with the simulator. Choosing {{tex:a \in \{0°, 90°\}}}, {{tex:b \in \{45°, 135°\}}} gives the CHSH value {{tex:2\sqrt2}} of Chapter 51: the Bell experiment as a circuit.

Three qubits, two kinds of entanglement

With three qubits, entanglement comes in inequivalent forms. Choose , {{tex:(|000\rangle + |111\rangle)/\sqrt2}}, or , {{tex:(|001\rangle + |010\rangle + |100\rangle)/\sqrt3}}. Now lose one qubit, trace it out. GHZ leaves the other two in a classical mixture of 00 and 11, with no entanglement at all. W leaves them still entangled, with concurrence 2/3. GHZ is fragile but maximally correlated; W is robust. No local operations, even with luck, turn one into the other: Wolfgang Dür, Guifré Vidal and Ignacio Cirac showed in 2000 these are genuinely different classes. Established
