How many qubits does it take to break RSA-2048? In 2012 the estimate was about a billion. In 2019 Craig Gidney and Martin Ekerå brought it to 20 million, running for 8 hours. In May 2025 Gidney brought it below one million, running for under a week. The hardware assumptions barely changed. The savings came from arithmetic, compilation and error-correction tricks, and you can trace every factor with a short chain of reasoning. Resource estimation is the skill of turning an algorithm into a bill of materials.

On the plate: a resource estimator. Its defaults are Gidney’s 2025 assumptions.

The pipeline

Five steps. (1) Logical counts: how many logical qubits {{tex:Q}} and how many Toffoli or T gates {{tex:N_T}} the algorithm needs, after compilation (Chapter 142). (2) Run time: non-Clifford gates are sequential steps, each limited by the code cycle time times the distance, or by the reaction time needed to decode and choose the next operation. (3) Distance: choose {{tex:d}} so that the total chance of any logical failure, about {{tex:Q\times(\text{rounds})\times p_L(d)}}, stays below 1%, with {{tex:p_L(d)\approx0.1\,(p/p_\text{th})^{(d+1)/2}}} and {{tex:p_\text{th}\approx1\%}}. (4) Footprint: about {{tex:2(d+1)^2}} physical qubits per logical qubit in the surface code, plus magic-state factories. (5) Check against the assumptions. Established

Run the estimator

Physical error {{tex:p =}} 10^-3.0, cycle time 1.0 µs, reaction time 10 µs, logical qubits 1400, Toffolis 10^9.8, storage in or .

With Gidney’s assumptions (0.1% error, 1 µs cycle, 10 µs reaction, about 1,400 logical qubits and {{tex:6.5\times10^9}} Toffolis) and dense storage, this simple model gives distance 31, about 1.2 million physical qubits and 2–3 days. Gidney’s detailed design, with yoked surface codes for idle qubits and magic-state cultivation (Chapter 140), lands under a million qubits and under a week. Halve the error rate and the footprint drops by almost half; double it and it doubles. Established for the method; the model is deliberately simplified.

Where the savings came from, and chemistry

From 20 million (2019) to under one million (2025): approximate modular arithmetic, which tolerates rare errors in the factoring computation; storing idle qubits in denser yoked codes; replacing distillation factories with cultivation; and accepting a longer run time. Michael Beverland and colleagues at Microsoft published in 2022 a systematic framework, now the Azure Quantum Resource Estimator, applying the same pipeline to chemistry and materials. Their conclusion: practical quantum advantage needs on the order of a million physical qubits with error rates near {{tex:10^{-4}}} to {{tex:10^{-3}}}, and fast gates. FeMoco, the iron–molybdenum cofactor of nitrogenase (Part 5), needs a few thousand logical qubits and billions of Toffolis in the best published estimates. Alexander Dalzell and colleagues’ 2023 survey collects end-to-end costs for many applications. Established for the estimates; Speculative for when hardware will meet them.
