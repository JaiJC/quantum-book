Almost nothing in quantum mechanics can be solved exactly. The hydrogen atom can; helium cannot. The harmonic oscillator can; a real chemical bond cannot. Yet the magnetic strength of the electron has been calculated and measured to agree to about one part in a trillion. The tool that made that possible is perturbation theory: start from a problem you can solve, and add the rest a little at a time.

On the plate: an oscillator whose walls have been made slightly steeper by an extra {{tex:\lambda x^4}}.

First order

Split the energy operator into a solved part and a small extra, {{tex:H = H_0 + \lambda V}}. Expand the energies and states in powers of {{tex:\lambda}} and match powers. The first correction to an energy is just the average of the extra term in the unperturbed state:

{{texd:E_n^{(1)} = \langle n|V|n\rangle}}

For the oscillator with {{tex:V = x^4}}, the ground state gives {{tex:\langle x^4\rangle = \tfrac34}} in natural units, so {{tex:E_0 \approx \tfrac12 + \tfrac34\lambda}}. Set {{tex:\lambda =}} 0.10. Second order

The state itself also changes, by mixing in other states, and that feeds back into the energy at the next order:

{{texd:E_n^{(2)} = \sum_{m\ne n}\frac{|\langle m|V|n\rangle|^2}{E_n - E_m}}}

Two rules follow at once. The ground state’s second-order shift is always downward, since every denominator is negative. And states close in energy push each other apart: level repulsion. For {{tex:x^4}} the sum gives {{tex:-\tfrac{21}{8}\lambda^2}}. On the plate, the exact energy is white, first order dashed, second order amber. For small {{tex:\lambda}} second order hugs the exact curve; as {{tex:\lambda}} grows it peels away.

When levels share an energy

If two unperturbed states have the same energy, the second-order formula divides by zero. The cure is to first find the right combinations: diagonalize {{tex:V}} within the degenerate group. Hydrogen’s {{tex:n = 2}} level holds 2s and three 2p states. An electric field {{tex:\mathcal E}} along {{tex:z}} connects only 2s and {{tex:2p_0}}, and their combinations shift by {{tex:\pm3ea_0\mathcal E}}: a shift linear in the field, unusual because ordinary atoms have no permanent dipole. Raise the field to 5.0 MV/m; the other two 2p states stay put.

Measuring electric fields inside proteins

The ground state has no degeneracy, so its first-order Stark shift is zero, and the leading effect is second order: {{tex:\Delta E = -\tfrac12\alpha\mathcal E^2}}, with the polarizability {{tex:\alpha}} built from the sum over states.

Molecular vibrations shift too, and that has become a practical tool. A carbonyl or nitrile group’s stretching frequency moves linearly with the electric field along its bond: the vibrational Stark effect. Steven Boxer’s group calibrated these probes and used them to measure the electric fields that enzymes apply to their substrates, which reach the order of 100 megavolts per centimetre, and found that stronger fields go with faster catalysis. Established

Fine structure, derived

Hydrogen’s fine structure from Chapter 38 is three perturbations added together: the relativistic correction to kinetic energy, spin–orbit coupling, and the Darwin term for s states. Each is of order {{tex:\alpha^2}} times the binding energy, and together they give

{{texd:\Delta E_{nj} = -\frac{13.6\ \text{eV}}{n^2}\cdot\frac{\alpha^2}{n^2}\left(\frac{n}{j+\frac12} - \frac34\right)}}

The energy depends only on {{tex:n}} and {{tex:j}}, so 2s1/2 and 2p1/2 stay together, exactly as Dirac’s equation says, until the Lamb shift separates them. The plate shows the {{tex:n = 2}} and {{tex:n = 3}} levels magnified about a hundred thousand times.

A series that never converges

Here is the surprise. Carl Bender and Tai Tsun Wu computed the {{tex:x^4}} series to high order in 1969: its coefficients are {{tex:\tfrac34, -\tfrac{21}{8}, \tfrac{333}{16}, -241.3, 3581, \dots}}, growing like a factorial. The series diverges for every {{tex:\lambda \ne 0}}. Freeman Dyson saw the reason in 1952 for QED: if the series converged for small positive {{tex:\lambda}}, it would also converge for small negative {{tex:\lambda}}, where the potential turns over and no stable ground state exists.

The plate adds the terms one by one at your {{tex:\lambda}}. The partial sums close in on the exact value, then swing away. The series is asymptotic: stop at the smallest term and you get an excellent answer; carry on and you get nonsense. QED’s series is believed to be the same, but its terms keep shrinking for about 137 orders, far more than anyone can compute. Established

What you now have

First and second-order perturbation theory, level repulsion, degenerate perturbation theory and the linear Stark effect, polarizability and vibrational Stark probes, fine structure as a sum of perturbations, and asymptotic series.
