Wind a long, thin coil of wire and run a current through it. The magnetic field is all inside; outside, there is none at all. Send electrons around the outside, never through, and recombine them. Their interference pattern shifts as you change the current. The electrons respond to a magnetic field they never touch. Yakir Aharonov and David Bohm predicted this in 1959, and Akira Tonomura’s team confirmed it beyond doubt in 1986.

How a charge feels a magnetic field

In Chapter 15 the magnetic field was written as {{tex:\mathbf B = \nabla\times\mathbf A}}, with {{tex:\mathbf A}} the vector potential. Quantum mechanics brings in the field through {{tex:\mathbf A}}, not {{tex:\mathbf B}}, by the rule called minimal coupling:

{{texd:H = \frac{(\mathbf p - q\mathbf A)^2}{2m} + q\phi}}

{{tex:\mathbf A}} is not unique: adding the gradient of any function {{tex:\chi}} leaves {{tex:\mathbf B}} unchanged. The Schrödinger equation stays the same if the wavefunction changes phase at the same time, {{tex:\psi \to e^{iq\chi/\hbar}\psi}}. This gauge invariance looks like bookkeeping here. In Part Five it becomes the principle from which the forces of the Standard Model are built. Established

Landau levels

A classical electron in a uniform field circles at the cyclotron frequency {{tex:\omega_c = eB/m}}. Quantum mechanically, the motion in the plane is a harmonic oscillator (Chapter 35), so the energies are

{{texd:E_n = \hbar\omega_c\left(n + \tfrac12\right)}}

These are Landau levels. Each is enormously degenerate: one state for every flux quantum {{tex:h/e}} passing through the sample, about {{tex:2.4\times10^{14}}} per square metre per tesla. Set the field to 10 T. The spacing grows with {{tex:B}} and each level holds more states. When a two-dimensional sheet of electrons fills a whole number of these levels, its Hall resistance locks to {{tex:h/ne^2}} with astonishing precision: the quantum Hall effect, which Part Four takes up.

Splitting spectral lines

In 1896 Pieter Zeeman put a sodium flame between the poles of a magnet and saw its yellow lines broaden. A magnetic moment {{tex:\boldsymbol\mu}} in a field has energy {{tex:-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B}}, so each level shifts by {{tex:g_J\,m_J\,\mu_B B}}, with the Bohr magneton {{tex:\mu_B = 5.788\times10^{-5}}} eV/T and the Landé factors of Chapter 41.

Raise the field to 1.0 T. Because the upper and lower levels have different {{tex:g_J}}, the D₁ line splits into four components and D₂ into six. This pattern was called anomalous, because the simple three-line splitting that classical theory predicted only appears for atoms with no net spin. Its explanation needed spin and {{tex:g_s = 2}}. Established

When the field wins

The Zeeman formula assumes the field is weak compared with spin–orbit coupling, so {{tex:\mathbf L}} and {{tex:\mathbf S}} stay locked into {{tex:\mathbf J}}. In a strong field, they decouple and each precesses about {{tex:\mathbf B}} separately, and the energies become {{tex:\mu_B B(m_\ell + 2m_s)}}: the Paschen–Back regime.

The plate solves the sodium 3p levels exactly for any field. Stretch the range to 60 T. At low field, two groups fan out with slopes {{tex:g_J m_J}}. Around 25 T, where {{tex:\mu_B B}} matches the spin–orbit energy, the levels reorganize into groups set by {{tex:m_\ell + 2m_s}}.

A field it never touches

Back to the coil. Outside it, {{tex:\mathbf B = 0}} but {{tex:\mathbf A}} is not zero; it circles the coil. An electron going round one side picks up a phase {{tex:\frac{e}{\hbar}\int\mathbf A\cdot d\mathbf l}}, and the difference between the two sides is {{tex:e\Phi/\hbar}}, set by the flux {{tex:\Phi}} inside, which neither path enters.

Change the enclosed flux to 0.25 flux quanta {{tex:h/e}}. The fringes slide by that fraction of a fringe. Tonomura’s group shielded a tiny ring magnet inside a superconductor, so no field could leak out and the electrons could not reach it, and still saw the shift. The vector potential, or more precisely the phase it produces around a loop, is physical. Established

Why does sodium’s D₂ line split into six?tap
The upper ²P₃/₂ level has four m_J values with g = 4/3, the lower ²S₁/₂ has two with g = 2; allowed jumps (Δm = 0, ±1) give six different energies.
What sets the degeneracy of a Landau level?tap
One state per flux quantum h/e through the sample: eB/h per unit area.
What does the Aharonov–Bohm effect show?tap
That electrons respond to the vector potential’s loop phase eΦ/ħ even where B = 0.
