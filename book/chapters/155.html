Demonstrated, as of late 2026

Logical qubits below threshold, with error falling as codes grow (superconducting, Google 2024–25; neutral atoms, Harvard–MIT–QuEra 2025). Two-qubit fidelities above 99.9% (trapped ions, Quantinuum Helios 2025). Arrays of thousands of physical qubits (neutral atoms, Caltech 2025). Sampling tasks beyond straightforward classical simulation, repeatedly contested (Chapter 127). Certified randomness (Chapter 134). Not yet demonstrated: a useful computation that is out of classical reach and verified; a logical qubit sustained through deep, universal, error-corrected computation; a topological qubit. The scorecard

Sort the platforms by . Ratings run from 0 (not shown) to 3 (leading), and are this book’s judgement from the sources in Chapters 144–148. No platform leads on everything: ions lead on fidelity and connectivity but are slow; superconducting circuits are fast but wired to neighbours and need millikelvin fridges; neutral atoms scale and move but cycle in milliseconds; photonics networks naturally but fights loss; spins promise manufacturing but are small so far; topological qubits remain unproven.

Roadmaps are promises: IBM’s Starling, 200 logical qubits and 100 million gates by 2029, and DARPA’s 2033 value test (Chapter 150). What would change this picture? A verified useful computation beyond classical reach; logical error rates of {{tex:10^{-6}}} or below sustained over many logical operations; or a clean demonstration of topological protection. Speculative for every roadmap date.

India

The National Quantum Mission, approved in April 2023, commits ₹6,003.65 crore from 2023–24 to 2030–31. It funds four thematic hubs: quantum computing at IISc Bengaluru, communication at IIT Madras with C-DOT, sensing and metrology at IIT Bombay, and materials and devices at IIT Delhi. Its targets include 50–1,000 physical-qubit machines, satellite-based secure communication over 2,000 km, and magnetometers and atomic clocks; in 2026 it reported a 1,000 km secure network (Chapter 153). Research groups at TIFR, RRI, IISER Pune and the IITs work across platforms, and startups such as QNu Labs (communication) and QpiAI (superconducting hardware) are building products.

For a biomedical engineer, the strongest bridges are quantum sensing (NV magnetometry and optically pumped magnetometers for brain and heart signals), quantum chemistry for drug discovery (Part 5), and the instrumentation skills every platform needs: microwave and optical engineering, cryogenics, control software and signal processing. Established for the mission’s structure; Speculative for its targets.
