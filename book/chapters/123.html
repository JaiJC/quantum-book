Training a variational circuit means walking downhill on an energy landscape. In 2018 Jarrod McClean and colleagues at Google showed that for generic, expressive circuits the landscape is almost perfectly flat: gradients are exponentially small in the number of qubits, everywhere except in a vanishing fraction of parameter space. To see the slope, you need exponentially many measurements. They called these regions barren plateaus, and they reshaped the field.

On the plate: the spread of gradients in random circuits, as qubits are added.

Why the landscape is flat

A circuit deep and random enough behaves like a random unitary (technically, a 2-design). Then the cost {{tex:\langle\psi(\theta)|O|\psi(\theta)\rangle}} concentrates around its average, just as the overlap of two random vectors in a huge space concentrates near zero. The mean gradient is zero and its variance shrinks like {{tex:1/2^n}} or faster. This is concentration of measure, the same effect that makes random high-dimensional vectors nearly orthogonal. Established

Global versus local cost

The plate shows gradient variances computed by exact simulation of a layered circuit of random {{tex:R_y}}, {{tex:R_z}} rotations and controlled-Z gates, with as many layers as qubits. Show , , . A global cost asks whether the whole register is in {{tex:|0\cdots0\rangle}}; its gradient variance falls from 0.037 at 2 qubits to 1.2 × 10⁻⁶ at 10. A local cost averages single-qubit measurements; it falls from 0.026 to 2.5 × 10⁻⁴, far more slowly. Marco Cerezo and colleagues proved in 2021 that local costs with shallow circuits avoid barren plateaus. Samson Wang and colleagues showed the same year that hardware noise causes plateaus of its own, whatever the cost function. Established

The twist: trainable may mean simulable

In 2024 two groups, Michael Ragone and colleagues and Enrico Fontana and colleagues, tied gradient variance to the dimension of the Lie algebra generated by the circuit’s gates: a small algebra means no plateau. But a small algebra also means the dynamics lives in a small subspace. In 2025 Cerezo and colleagues argued that most provably plateau-free models can then be simulated classically, possibly after collecting some data from the quantum device. If true in general, the structure that makes a model trainable is the same structure that removes its quantum advantage. Exceptions, and how far the argument extends, are actively debated. Debated

What you now have

Barren plateaus as concentration of measure, the exponential decay of gradient variance, the advantage of local costs, noise-induced plateaus, the Lie-algebraic theory, and the trainability–simulability tension.
