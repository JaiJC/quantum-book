Put Maxwell’s equations in empty space, with no charges anywhere, and they still have something to say. A changing magnetic field makes an electric field; a changing electric field, thanks to Maxwell’s extra term, makes a magnetic field. Each keeps the other going, and the pair can travel on their own. Maxwell worked out how fast, and got the speed of light.

This chapter shows how light falls out of the equations, what it carries, and then how the same equations predict that every atom should destroy itself in a hundred-billionth of a second. That failure is where quantum physics begins.

The wave equation hiding in Maxwell’s

Take the curl of Faraday’s law and use Ampère–Maxwell to replace the curl of {{tex:\mathbf B}}. In empty space the result is the wave equation of Chapter 5 for every component of the field:

{{texd:\nabla^2\mathbf E = \mu_0\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial^2\mathbf E}{\partial t^2}, \qquad c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}} = 299{,}792{,}458\ \text{m/s}}}

The two constants come from tabletop measurements of electric and magnetic forces. In 1856 Wilhelm Weber and Rudolf Kohlrausch measured their ratio and found a speed of about {{tex:3.1\times10^8}} m/s; Hippolyte Fizeau had timed light itself in 1849 with a spinning toothed wheel at about {{tex:3.1\times10^8}} m/s too. Maxwell concluded that light is an electromagnetic wave. Established

What a light wave looks like

In a plane wave, {{tex:\mathbf E}} and {{tex:\mathbf B}} oscillate in step, at right angles to each other and to the direction of travel. The plate shows them in perspective; to see that the two fields stay perpendicular. Their sizes are tied: {{tex:B = E/c}}.

Heinrich Hertz made and detected such waves with sparks and wire loops in 1887–88, at radio frequencies, and measured their speed. The visible light you read by is the same thing with a wavelength a million times shorter.

Light carries energy and push

The energy flowing through each square metre per second is the Poynting vector, {{tex:\mathbf S = \mathbf E\times\mathbf B/\mu_0}}. Sunlight at Earth delivers about 1361 W per square metre, which means an electric field swinging with an amplitude of about 1000 V/m.

Light also carries momentum: absorbing it pushes with a pressure {{tex:S/c}}, and reflecting it twice that. For sunlight on a mirror that is 9 micropascals, a force of 9 newtons on a square kilometre. Tiny, but in space it never stops, and solar sails such as JAXA’s IKAROS (2010) have flown on it. Shaking a charge makes light

A charge moving steadily carries its field along. A charge that accelerates sends out a ripple. The power radiated is the Larmor formula:

{{texd:P = \frac{q^2 a^2}{6\pi\varepsilon_0 c^3}}}

A charge oscillating up and down radiates most strongly sideways and not at all along its line of motion, the doughnut pattern on the plate. Every radio antenna has this pattern.

The atom that should not exist

Now the crisis. In the planetary picture of the atom, an electron circles the nucleus. Circling is accelerating, so the electron must radiate, lose energy and spiral inward. Use the Larmor formula and you can work out how long the fall takes, from the size of a hydrogen atom down to the nucleus:

{{texd:t = \frac{a_0^3}{4 r_e^2 c} \approx 1.6\times10^{-11}\ \text{s}}}

Here {{tex:a_0}} is the atom’s radius and {{tex:r_e}} the classical electron radius. ; the plate slows time about five hundred billion times so you can watch.

Atoms have lasted 13.8 billion years. Classical physics is not slightly wrong here; it is wrong by more than 28 powers of ten. Fixing that is the business of Part One.

The freedom in the potentials

The fields can be written using potentials, {{tex:\mathbf B = \nabla\times\mathbf A}} and {{tex:\mathbf E = -\nabla\phi - \partial_t\mathbf A}}. The potentials are not unique: change them by {{tex:\mathbf A \to \mathbf A + \nabla\chi}} and {{tex:\phi \to \phi - \partial_t\chi}}, for any function {{tex:\chi}}, and the fields do not change at all. This gauge freedom looks like a nuisance. In Part Two it becomes the organizing principle of every force in the Standard Model.

DeeperCheck: {{tex:\nabla\times\nabla\chi = 0}}, so {{tex:\mathbf B}} is unchanged; and {{tex:-\nabla(\phi - \partial_t\chi) - \partial_t(\mathbf A + \nabla\chi) = -\nabla\phi - \partial_t\mathbf A}}, so {{tex:\mathbf E}} is unchanged.

The potentials φ and A, but not the fields E and B, so nothing measurable.
