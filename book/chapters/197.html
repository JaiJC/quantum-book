Marcus theory (Chapter 163) treats the environment’s motions classically. That works when the relevant vibrations are slow and thermally excited. But high-frequency molecular vibrations, carbon–carbon and carbon–oxygen stretches with quanta far above {{tex:k_BT}}, cannot be treated classically, and they change the inverted region dramatically. In 2024 a trapped-ion experiment simulated electron transfer with a tunable environment, reproducing these regimes on demand. This chapter shows the physics and the experiment.

Beyond classical Marcus

Treat the slow environment classically, with reorganization energy {{tex:\lambda_s}}, and add one high-frequency vibration of energy {{tex:\hbar\omega}} with Huang–Rhys factor {{tex:S}} (how much it is displaced by the transfer). The electron can now land in the product state with {{tex:n}} quanta of vibration excited, each channel with Franck–Condon weight {{tex:e^{-S}S^n/n!}}:

{{texd:k\propto\sum_{n}\frac{e^{-S}S^n}{n!}\exp\Big[-\frac{(\Delta G+\lambda_s+n\hbar\omega)^2}{4\lambda_sk_BT}\Big]}}

This Marcus–Levich–Jortner form, developed by Veniamin Levich, Joshua Jortner and others, softens the inverted region: very exothermic transfers dump their excess energy into vibrational quanta instead of climbing a barrier. Formally it is a spin-boson model, a two-level system coupled to a bath of oscillators. Established

Compare the rates

Solvent reorganization {{tex:\lambda_s =}} 0.40 eV, vibration {{tex:\hbar\omega =}} 0.18 eV (a C=C stretch is about 0.18 eV), Huang–Rhys factor {{tex:S =}} 1.0. The plate plots log rate against driving force for classical Marcus (white) and with the quantum mode (amber). In the normal region they agree; in the inverted region the quantum curve falls far more slowly. In December 2024 Visal So, Guido Pagano and colleagues at Rice University published in Science Advances a trapped-ion simulation of electron transfer, encoding donor and acceptor in an ion’s spin and the environment in its motion, with dissipation engineered by laser cooling, and measured transfer rates across normal and inverted regimes. Established for the demonstration.

The critique

A single spin coupled to one damped mode is classically solvable, and Marcus–Levich–Jortner theory already describes most experiments. The value of analog simulators lies in regimes classical methods find hard: strong, structured coupling to many modes, non-Markovian memory, and multi-site networks where coherent and incoherent transfer compete. Those regimes do matter for biology, for instance in photosynthetic reaction centres and multi-cofactor chains, but no simulation has yet produced a biological result classical methods could not. Speculative for advantage.
