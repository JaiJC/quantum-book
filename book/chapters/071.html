Heat water in a sealed vessel to 374 °C under 218 atmospheres and the difference between liquid and vapour vanishes. Near that critical point, the density fluctuates on every scale at once, and the way it does so is described by exactly the same numbers as a magnet losing its magnetism, or a binary alloy unmixing. The microscopic details are completely different; the behaviour is identical. Kenneth Wilson explained why in 1971, with an idea that also explains why quantum field theory works at all: the renormalization group.

Throw away the small scales

Wilson’s idea: describe a system at some resolution, then average away the finest details and ask what theory describes what is left. For a lattice of spins, group them into 2 × 2 blocks and replace each block by its majority vote. The new lattice has the same kind of description, with different couplings. Repeat. The couplings flow.

An exact flow in one dimension

For a chain of spins with coupling {{tex:K = J/k_BT}}, summing out every second spin gives a chain with half as many spins and a new coupling:

{{texd:\tanh K' = \tanh^2K}}

Since {{tex:\tanh K < 1}}, {{tex:K'}} is always smaller than {{tex:K}}. Every starting point flows to {{tex:K = 0}}, infinite temperature, so a one-dimensional magnet never orders at any nonzero temperature, a result Ising found in 1925 by other means. The plate iterates the map from {{tex:K = 1}}: 0.66, 0.35, 0.11, 0.01.

Relevant and irrelevant

Near a fixed point, small changes to the couplings either grow under the flow, relevant; shrink, irrelevant; or stay put to first order, marginal. Temperature is relevant at the critical point, which is why you must tune it exactly. Almost every microscopic detail, the shape of the molecules, the lattice structure, is irrelevant: it shrinks away. That is universality. Water near its critical point and a uniaxial magnet share the critical exponents of the three-dimensional Ising class, such as {{tex:\beta \approx 0.326}}, because they flow to the same fixed point.

The same story in particle physics

Run the flow backwards and you get the quantum field theories of Chapter 70. The running of {{tex:\alpha}} is a renormalization group flow, with energy in place of block size. Renormalizable couplings are the marginal and relevant ones; non-renormalizable terms are irrelevant, which is why low-energy physics can ignore them. And the reason a field theory with a cutoff gives cutoff-independent predictions is the same reason boiling water ignores its molecules. Wilson received the 1982 Nobel Prize for this unification of critical phenomena and quantum fields.
