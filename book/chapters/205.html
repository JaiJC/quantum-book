After twenty-three chapters of Part 5, here is the ledger. Every quantum-for-biology claim in this book falls into one of three columns: demonstrated on real hardware, estimated by careful resource calculations for machines not yet built, or hoped, proposed without either. For each, the ledger records the size, the best classical comparison, and a verdict, with the date it was last checked. Keep it, and update it as the field moves.

On the plate: the claims ledger.

How to read the ledger

“Demonstrated” means it ran on quantum hardware or a quantum sensor and produced the stated result. It does not mean advantage: almost every demonstrated quantum-computing result in biology is small enough to solve classically, often trivially. “Estimated” means a peer-reviewed or carefully documented calculation of logical and physical resources. “Hoped” means a proposal without a resource estimate or a hardware result. The verdict column applies the tests of Chapter 180 and the advantage checklist of Chapter 150. Established as method.

The ledger

Filter: . One pattern stands out. The only quantum technologies already delivering value to biology and medicine are sensors: optically pumped magnetometers, NV centres, hyperpolarized MRI. Every quantum-computing demonstration so far is a proof of principle at classically tractable scale. The strongest computing case remains strongly correlated active sites, with resource estimates in the millions of physical qubits, and falling. What would move entries

An entry moves from “estimated” to “demonstrated advantage” when a fault-tolerant machine computes an energy for a strongly correlated active site that the best classical methods cannot match, verified independently. An entry moves from “hoped” to “estimated” when someone publishes an end-to-end resource count including data loading and readout. And entries move backwards when better classical algorithms appear, which has happened repeatedly (Chapters 121, 127, 150). The ledger is a tool for honest updating, not a scoreboard. Established as method.

What you now have

A dated ledger of quantum-for-biology claims in three columns, the observation that sensing already delivers while computing remains at proof-of-principle scale, and the criteria for moving an entry.
