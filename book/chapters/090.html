The Higgs mass is 125 GeV. Quantum corrections to it, from virtual top quarks for instance, grow with the square of the highest energy at which the Standard Model still holds. If that is the Planck scale, the corrections are larger than the observed mass by a factor of about {{tex:10^{32}}}, and must cancel against the bare value to thirty-two decimal places. Most physicists found that implausible, and for forty years almost every proposal to explain it predicted new particles at the LHC. None has appeared. Whether that means nature is fine-tuned, or the question was wrong, is the sharpest open debate in particle physics.

The hierarchy problem

The top-quark loop shifts the Higgs mass-squared by about {{tex:-\frac{3y_t^2}{8\pi^2}\Lambda^2}}, where {{tex:\Lambda}} is the scale where new physics takes over. Set {{tex:\Lambda =}} 4.0 (log₁₀ GeV). At 10 TeV the correction is already 200 times {{tex:m_h^2}}; at the Planck scale, {{tex:10^{32}}} times. Scalar masses, unlike fermion or gauge-boson masses, are not protected by any symmetry of the Standard Model. Naturalness says such cancellations should not be needed, so new physics should appear near a TeV. Debated

Three proposed answers

Supersymmetry pairs every fermion with a boson and vice versa; their loops cancel, and the Higgs mass is protected if the partners are not far above a TeV. Extra dimensions: if gravity spreads into extra dimensions (Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos and Dvali, 1998) or a warped fifth dimension (Randall and Sundrum, 1999), the true fundamental scale could be near a TeV, removing the hierarchy. Composite Higgs: the Higgs is not elementary but a bound state, like the pion (Chapter 76), so its mass is set by a new strong force. Choose one: , , . Unification

Run the three gauge couplings upward with their one-loop β-functions (Chapters 70 and 79). In the Standard Model they approach each other near {{tex:10^{13}}}–{{tex:10^{17}}} GeV but do not meet. Add supersymmetric partners at 1 TeV and the slopes change; the three lines meet, within uncertainties, near {{tex:2\times10^{16}}} GeV. Toggle: or . This was one of the strongest hints for supersymmetry and grand unification. It still is a hint; it is not evidence that the partners are at a TeV. Debated

What the LHC has said

Searches at 13 TeV exclude gluinos below about 2.3 TeV and top-quark partners below about 1.3 TeV in the simplest scenarios, Kaluza–Klein gravitons and new heavy gauge bosons below several TeV, and push composite-Higgs scales well above a TeV through precise Higgs coupling measurements. Each limit depends on assumptions, and compressed or unusual spectra can hide. But the simple, natural versions of each idea are in serious trouble. Established as limits.

Is naturalness wrong?

Defenders say naturalness worked before: the charm quark was predicted because a cancellation would otherwise have been needed (Chapter 83), and the pion mass difference predicted the ρ meson’s scale. Critics say the argument rests on assigning probabilities to parameter values with no theory of how they are chosen. Some invoke a multiverse in which our fine-tuned values are selected by the need for complex chemistry; others suspect the cutoff picture itself misleads. Gian Giudice called the situation, in 2017, a “dawn of the post-naturalness era”. No consensus exists. Debated
