Why is energy conserved? Not because someone measured it carefully, though they did. Energy is conserved because the laws of physics are the same today as they will be tomorrow. Momentum is conserved because they are the same here as over there; angular momentum, because they don’t care which way you face; electric charge, because the phase of a quantum field can be turned without anyone noticing. In 1918 Emmy Noether proved that every continuous symmetry of this kind comes with a conserved quantity, and that the connection runs both ways.

Break the symmetry, lose the conservation

A particle moves in the bowl {{tex:V = \tfrac12[(1 + \varepsilon)x^2 + y^2]}}. When {{tex:\varepsilon = 0}} the bowl is round, the Lagrangian is unchanged by rotations, and the angular momentum {{tex:L_z}} is constant. Squash the bowl with {{tex:\varepsilon =}} 0.20. Now {{tex:L_z}}, plotted in amber, swings back and forth, even reversing sign. The energy, in white, stays perfectly flat: the bowl still does not change with time.

This is Noether’s theorem in miniature. A conserved quantity is not a separate law; it is the shadow of a symmetry. Established

The theorem

Suppose a small change of the field, {{tex:\phi \to \phi + \epsilon\,\delta\phi}}, changes the Lagrangian density at most by a total derivative, {{tex:\delta\mathcal L = \epsilon\,\partial_\mu K^\mu}}. Then, on any solution of the equations of motion,

{{texd:j^\mu = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\,\delta\phi - K^\mu \qquad\text{obeys}\qquad \partial_\mu j^\mu = 0}}

A current with zero divergence means the charge {{tex:Q = \int j^0\,d^3x}} in any region changes only by what flows through its boundary. The plate shows it: the amount in the box changes only as current crosses the edge, never by appearing or vanishing inside.

DeeperCompute {{tex:\delta\mathcal L}} by the chain rule, use the Euler–Lagrange equation of Chapter 58 to replace {{tex:\partial\mathcal L/\partial\phi}}, and the result collects into {{tex:\partial_\mu(\ldots)}}. Setting it equal to {{tex:\partial_\mu K^\mu}} gives the theorem.

Energy, momentum and the stress tensor

Shifting all of spacetime, {{tex:x^\nu \to x^\nu + a^\nu}}, is four symmetries at once, and they give four conserved currents, gathered into the stress–energy tensor:

{{texd:T^{\mu\nu} = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\,\partial^\nu\phi - \eta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L, \qquad \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0}}

{{tex:T^{00}}} is the energy density, {{tex:T^{0i}}} the momentum density, and {{tex:T^{ij}}} the flow of momentum, which is pressure and stress. Choose or to see its pattern. In general relativity this same tensor is what curves spacetime.

Phase symmetry and charge

A complex field can have its phase turned everywhere at once, {{tex:\phi \to e^{i\alpha}\phi}}, and the Lagrangian {{tex:|\partial\phi|^2 - m^2|\phi|^2}} does not notice. Noether gives the current of Chapter 56, {{tex:j^\mu = i(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi - \phi\,\partial^\mu\phi^*)}}; for the Dirac field it is {{tex:\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi}}. Conservation of electric charge is this symmetry. In Chapter 74, demanding that the phase may be turned differently at each point will force the photon to exist.

When energy is not conserved

The theorem works both ways: no symmetry, no guarantee. An expanding universe is not the same today as tomorrow, and light crossing it loses energy. The cosmic microwave background was released when the universe was 1,100 times smaller; each photon then carried about 0.26 eV and now carries about a thousandth of that. Where did the energy go? Nowhere: in an expanding spacetime there is in general no conserved total energy to account for. Energy is still conserved locally, {{tex:\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0}}, but a global total needs a time symmetry that the universe does not have. Established, though how to phrase it is a matter of taste.
