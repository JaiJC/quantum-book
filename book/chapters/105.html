How different are two quantum states? There is a precise answer, and it is operational: exactly as different as your best chance of telling them apart in a single shot. The trace distance captures it. A second measure, fidelity, captures how close they are, and an entropy captures how mixed each one is. These three quantities run through every later chapter on error correction, benchmarking and cryptography.

On the plate: two states in the Bloch ball, with their distance, fidelity and the best odds of distinguishing them.

Von Neumann entropy

The quantum analogue of Shannon’s entropy (Chapter 95) is {{tex:S(\rho) = -\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho\log_2\rho = -\sum\lambda_i\log_2\lambda_i}}, over the eigenvalues of {{tex:\rho}}. Pure states have {{tex:S = 0}}; the maximally mixed qubit has 1 bit. Benjamin Schumacher showed in 1995 that {{tex:S}} is the number of qubits per copy needed to compress a quantum source, as {{tex:H}} counts bits for a classical one. The relative entropy {{tex:S(\rho\|\sigma) = \mathrm{Tr}\,\rho(\log\rho - \log\sigma)}} measures how distinguishable {{tex:\rho}} is from {{tex:\sigma}} over many copies, and never increases under any channel. Established

DeeperStrong subadditivity, {{tex:S(\rho_{ABC}) + S(\rho_B) \le S(\rho_{AB}) + S(\rho_{BC})}}, proved by Elliott Lieb and Mary Beth Ruskai in 1973, is the deepest inequality in quantum information. It follows from the monotonicity of relative entropy under partial trace, applied to {{tex:\rho_{ABC}}} against {{tex:\rho_A\otimes\rho_{BC}}}.

Trace distance

Define {{tex:D(\rho, \sigma) = \tfrac12\|\rho - \sigma\|_1}}, half the sum of the absolute eigenvalues of the difference. For qubits it is half the Euclidean distance between Bloch vectors. Its meaning: the best single-shot probability of telling them apart, given each with probability one half, is {{tex:\tfrac12(1 + D)}}, the Helstrom bound of Chapter 101 in general form. Move the states: lengths 1.00 and 0.80, angle between them 60°. Trace distance can only shrink under a channel: noise makes states harder to tell apart, never easier.

Fidelity

Uhlmann’s fidelity, {{tex:F(\rho, \sigma) = \left(\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt\rho\,\sigma\sqrt\rho}\right)^2}}, reduces to {{tex:|\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|^2}} for pure states. For qubits it has the closed form {{tex:\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\sigma) + 2\sqrt{\det\rho\det\sigma}}}. Distance and fidelity bound each other, by the Fuchs–van de Graaf inequalities:

{{texd:1 - \sqrt F \;\le\; D \;\le\; \sqrt{1 - F}}}

with the upper bound an equality for pure states. Gate and state fidelities quoted for hardware, 99.9% and so on, are these numbers (Chapter 150). Established
