Picture a mattress: a grid of springs, one at every point, each tied to its neighbours. Press one point and a ripple spreads. Now make the springs smaller and closer together, without limit. What is left is a field, a number at every point of space that wobbles and passes its wobble on. Every quantum field in the Standard Model starts as one of these, written down with a single line, its Lagrangian density.

On the plate: a chain of masses joined by springs. Increase the number of masses and the chain becomes a continuous string.

From beads to a field

Take {{tex:N}} beads of mass {{tex:M}} spaced {{tex:a}} apart, joined by springs of stiffness {{tex:\kappa}}. Each bead obeys Newton’s law with forces from its two neighbours. Let {{tex:a \to 0}} with the mass per length {{tex:\mu = M/a}} and the tension {{tex:T = \kappa a}} fixed. The bead displacements {{tex:q_i(t)}} become a field {{tex:\phi(x, t)}}, and the difference between neighbours becomes a derivative. The equation of motion becomes the wave equation of Chapter 5, with speed {{tex:\sqrt{T/\mu}}}.

Use 16 beads. With few beads the pulse breaks into jitter; with many it travels cleanly as a continuous wave.

Lagrangian density

For the beads, the Lagrangian of Chapter 12 is a sum over beads of kinetic minus potential energy. In the limit the sum becomes an integral, {{tex:L = \int\mathcal L\,dx}}, and the action is {{tex:S = \int\mathcal L\,d^4x}}. For a relativistic scalar field the simplest choice is

{{texd:\mathcal L = \tfrac12\,\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi - \tfrac12 m^2\phi^2}}

Demanding {{tex:\delta S = 0}} for all variations gives the field version of the Euler–Lagrange equation, {{tex:\partial_\mu\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)} = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi}}}, which here is Klein–Gordon, Chapter 56. The mass term is a spring tying each bead to the ground. Established

What the mass term does

A wave of wavenumber {{tex:k}} on the bead chain has frequency {{tex:\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + \frac{4\kappa}{M}\sin^2(ka/2)}}, where {{tex:\omega_0}} comes from the springs to the ground. Set {{tex:\omega_0 =}} 1.0. For small {{tex:k}} the curve becomes {{tex:\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + c^2k^2}}: exactly {{tex:E^2 = m^2 + p^2}} with {{tex:E = \hbar\omega}}. The grid spacing shows only at wavenumbers near {{tex:\pi/a}}. Whether real space has a smallest spacing is unknown; Part Five returns to it.

Three Lagrangians for the whole Standard Model

Every field you will meet is built from three kinds of term.

{{texd:\begin{gathered}\mathcal L_{\text{scalar}} = \tfrac12(\partial\phi)^2 - \tfrac12m^2\phi^2 \\ \mathcal L_{\text{Dirac}} = \bar\psi\,(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu - m)\,\psi \\ \mathcal L_{\text{Maxwell}} = -\tfrac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} - j^\mu A_\mu\end{gathered}}}

Vary each and you recover Klein–Gordon, Dirac (Chapter 57) and Maxwell’s four equations (Chapter 15), with {{tex:F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu}} and {{tex:\bar\psi = \psi^\dagger\gamma^0}}. The plate shows a small change to a field configuration and the change in action it produces; on a solution, every small change leaves the action unchanged to first order. Interactions are extra products of fields, such as {{tex:\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu}}, which couples electrons to light (Chapter 67).

Why Lagrangians

Writing equations of motion directly would also work. Lagrangians win for three reasons. Symmetry is visible: if {{tex:\mathcal L}} is unchanged by a transformation, so is the physics, and Noether’s theorem (next chapter) turns each symmetry into a conservation law. Relativity is automatic: if {{tex:\mathcal L}} is a Lorentz scalar, the equations are covariant. And quantization starts from it, through canonical momenta (Chapter 60) or the path integral of Chapter 53, where {{tex:e^{iS}}} weighs each field configuration.

What you now have

Fields as limits of coupled oscillators, the Lagrangian density and the field Euler–Lagrange equation, the mass term as a spring to the ground, the scalar, Dirac and Maxwell Lagrangians, and why physics is written this way.
